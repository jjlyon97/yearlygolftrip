San Diego

Torrey Pines on the cliffs, sixty more courses inland, and weather that never argues.

A US Open course you can book online for a municipal rate, a beach town that works for non-golfers, and the most reliable golf weather in America. San Diego asks less of a trip than almost anywhere on this list and gives back more than people expect.

Torrey Pines is a municipal course. That fact never stops being remarkable: two US Opens, an annual Tour event, and a clifftop setting above the Pacific, on land owned by the city and bookable online. The South Course is the championship one — longer, tougher, and stretched considerably before 2008 — but plenty of locals prefer the North for its views and its gentler treatment of ordinary golfers. The catch is the tee sheet, which opens on a rolling window and empties immediately; the resort package at the Lodge is the reliable way in. Beyond Torrey, San Diego is deep rather than spectacular: Aviara and Maderas are excellent, Barona Creek plays firm and fast, and Coronado Municipal gives you bay views for less than a cart fee elsewhere. The weather never argues, and non-golfers have an actual city.

Getting there

Fly intoSan Diego (SAN), 20–40 min

San Diego International sits five minutes from downtown, and nothing is more than an hour away once you land.

Access: Torrey Pines is municipal — book online or take a resident with you

Why go

  • Torrey Pines South at a municipal rate
  • Genuinely year-round golf
  • Beaches and a real city for the non-golfers

Watch out for

  • Torrey South tee times go fast — book the moment the window opens
  • Marine layer can sit on the coast until midday
